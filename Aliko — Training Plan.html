Why the rotation

Week A and Week B use the same main lifts (bench, squat, pull-up/row) so progress is easy to track — but the rep ranges and accessory work shift, so your body keeps getting a slightly new stimulus instead of adapting and stalling out like before.

The progression rule

This is the part that actually makes you grow, not just the exercise list. If you hit the top of the rep range on every set for a lift, add a little weight next time you do that lift — smallest plate increment available. If you don't hit the top of the range, repeat the same weight next time. That's it. Log your weights somewhere (notes app is fine) so you know when to bump it.

4-week checkpoint

After running Week A and Week B twice each (4 weeks total), check in properly — how the lifts felt, what's gone up, what's stalled. That's when the plan gets adjusted into a new block, not before.

Deload: after finishing that first 4-week block, take one lighter week before starting the next — cut sets by about a third on everything. This is what keeps the plan sustainable over months, not just weeks.

The real issue is usually frequency, not food type

If you're not eating much, the fastest fix is adding one more meal or snack somewhere in your day — not overhauling what you eat. Little and often beats trying to force down huge plates.

Not a meal plan: this is direction, not a prescription. If eating enough stays genuinely hard, that's worth a chat with a doctor or dietitian — not something to force through training alone.

Sleep

Recovery happens during sleep, not the workout — if sleep's rough, the training won't pay off as well no matter how good the plan is. Try these for a couple weeks straight, not just when you remember:

  • Same wake-up time daily

    Even on rest days. This does more for sleep quality than almost anything else.

  • Phone down 30 min before bed

    Put it across the room if you can — charging it away from the bed removes the temptation entirely.

  • No caffeine after mid-afternoon

    Even if you don't feel wired, it can still be affecting how deep you sleep.

If it's still bad after a few weeks of actually sticking to this, that's worth mentioning to a doctor — not something to just push through.

Water

Carry a bottle and sip through the day rather than chugging it all at once. More on training days, especially since you're active with track on top of lifting.

Protein shake — Monday, Tuesday, Thursday, Saturday

Those are your training days on this schedule. Have it within an hour after the session — it's an easy way to get protein in without forcing down a big meal. Look for a halal-certified whey or plant-based option. Skip it Wednesday, Friday, and Sunday — get that protein from food instead on rest days. If you shift your training days around, shift the shake days with them.

This week's checklist

Taps save on this phone — check off each day. 🥤 only applies Mon / Tue / Thu / Sat — leave it blank on rest days.

  • Control the eccentric

    1–2 seconds on the way down for every main and supplemental lift. That's where most of the growth signal actually comes from — don't rush it.

  • Track reps in reserve (RIR)

    After each set, note how many more reps you could've done. Aim to leave 1–3 in the tank on most sets — this is what tells us when to add weight.

  • Why not 6 days a week

    Muscle grows during recovery, not during the workout itself. Training this hard 6x/week doesn't leave enough time to actually rebuild — that's almost certainly why you kept stalling before.

  • Check-ins

    We'll go over how sessions felt and adjust from there — send video if a lift feels off so we can fix form before it becomes a habit.
